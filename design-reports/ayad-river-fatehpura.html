A — Project Scope and Source Files
FATEHPURA–BHUWANA — THE DESIGN PROBLEM: The Fatehpura–Bhuwana Road crosses the Ayad River at a point downstream of Fateh Sagar Lake's spillway. During a significant monsoon, the lake overtops its weir and the Ayad swells rapidly, carrying a discharge that is partly determined by the lake's storage characteristics and partly by direct catchment runoff. The HFL at this crossing is therefore a composite flood — lake spill plus catchment contribution — and can be significantly higher than what Manning's equation applied to the natural channel alone would suggest. A high-level bridge is specified here (rather than submersible) because the road connects the Fatehpura residential zone to Bhuwana, an emerging commercial and educational precinct, and year-round trafficability is essential for commuter and emergency traffic. The presence of a dedicated LLCALpier1 XLS workbook — rather than simply using a tabulated pier reaction from standard tables — reflects the design engineer's rigour in computing the governing vehicle arrangement on this multi-lane crossing where transverse eccentricity significantly amplifies pier loading.
PROJECT IDENTITY: The Fatehpura–Bhuwana Road high-level bridge on the Ayad River is distinguished by the presence of the live-load computation workbook "LLCALpier1 HIGH LEVEL BRIDGE ACROSS AYAD RIVER ON FATEHPURA BHUWANA ROAD.xls" — a dedicated pier-line live-load calculation file using the standard IRC influence-line method. The Ayad at this location receives outflow from Fateh Sagar Lake, so the HFL is partly regulated by the lake spill level. The design hydraulics must account for this lacustrine influence on the flood level.

B — Live Load and BEDACH Stability Narrative
LIVE LOAD CALCULATION (IRC:6-2016 Cl. 204 — LLCALpier1 Method): The LLCALpier1 XLS uses the standard influence-line approach to find the worst-case vehicle position for maximum pier reaction. For a two-lane bridge with IRC Class-A or 70R loading, the governing vehicle is placed at the critical position determined by the influence-line ordinates. The maximum unfactored pier reaction, including impact factor per IRC:6-2016 Table 1, is the live-load input to all nine BEDACH stability cases. The braking/longitudinal load is 20% of the live load (or as per IRC:6 Cl. 211, whichever governs) and creates a horizontal destabilising action at the deck level. The LLCALpier1 XLS must also confirm that the transverse eccentricity of the vehicle arrangement produces the governing overturning moment.
FREEBOARD CHECK (IRC:5-2015): The Ayad River at Fatehpura is downstream of Fateh Sagar Lake. The design HFL must be obtained from the Udaipur municipal authority or the lake regulation authority, accounting for the lake's FSL and spillway capacity. The IRC:5-2015 minimum freeboard (dependent on design discharge tier) must be measured from the Design Water Level (HFL + Molesworth afflux) to the soffit of the deck slab. This check is mandatory for high-level bridge classification and must appear as an explicit calculation in the design file.

C — Abutment Design (Type 1 or 2 — Confirm from Cross Section XLS)
ABUTMENT (IRC:78-1983) — TYPE TO BE CONFIRMED: The Fatehpura–Bhuwana Road crosses the Ayad River downstream of Fateh Sagar Lake. The abutment height H depends on the approach embankment required to carry the deck above the regulated HFL. If H ≤ 4.5 m, a Type 1 gravity abutment (mass concrete) applies; if H > 4.5 m, a Type 2 cantilever RCC abutment (IRC:78-1983 + IRC:112-2015) is required. The cross section.xls (Git LFS) and the hydraulic Calculations AYAD BRIDGE.doc together define the required deck soffit level and thus the approach fill height. The abutment design must be confirmed by a dedicated Abutment XLS and cross-referenced to the LLCALpier1 XLS for the longitudinal braking force acting on the abutment cap.
