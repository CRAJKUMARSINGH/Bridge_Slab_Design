A — Project Context: Banas River HFL Bridge
THE BANAS AT DESIGN FLOOD — SCOUR PHYSICS: The Banas River drains approximately 45,000 km² of eastern Rajasthan before joining the Chambal near Ranthambore. At the design crossing, the bed material is predominantly sandy alluvium (median grain size d₅₀ ≈ 0.30–0.80 mm), giving a Lacey silt factor f = 1.76√d₅₀ in the range 0.96–1.57. This silt factor directly controls the design scour depth via Lacey's formula: d_sm = 0.473 × (Q/f)^(1/3) — where Q is in cumecs. For a large Banas flood, d_sm can easily reach 5–8 m, and the IRC:78-1983 design scour depth (= 2.0 × d_sm for open channels) can require foundations 10–16 m below the mean bed level. This explains why the R-1 open foundation on the sandy Banas River bed is the most geotechnically critical check in the entire project: if the founding level is not proved to lie below the IRC:78-1983 scour depth, the open foundation will be undermined and the pier will tilt or collapse. No other check in the BEDACH sequence is as consequential for long-term safety.
PROJECT IDENTITY: The Banas River is one of the principal rivers of Rajasthan, draining the eastern Aravalli slope. At the design crossing, the river has a wide sandy bed with significant seasonal variation in flood level. The "HFL" in the filename refers to the Highest Flood Level designation, confirming this is a high-level bridge designed to pass above the design flood. The "R-1 variant" designation suggests this is one of multiple design alternatives — the R-1 being the open-foundation option as opposed to a piled or well-foundation alternative. Open foundations on sandy river beds require careful scour analysis per IRC:78-1983.
OPEN FOUNDATION ON SANDY RIVER BED: Unlike rock-bearing or pile foundations, an open foundation on the Banas River bed is susceptible to scour. The IRC:78-1983 design scour depth must be computed using the Lacey method for the sandy bed (silt factor f = 1.76√m, where m is the median grain diameter in mm). The foundation must be taken to a level at least 1.33 times the design scour depth below the HFL (or 2.0m below the low-water channel bed, whichever is deeper). This is the most critical check for the Banas River open foundation.

B — BEDACH Stability and Freeboard Narrative
BEDACH LOAD SEQUENCE — HIGH-LEVEL BRIDGE: For a high-level bridge, the dead load includes the superstructure (slab, wearing coat, parapet, crash barrier) in addition to the substructure. The wind load on the superstructure elevation is more significant than for a submersible bridge (because the deck is above HFL and exposed). The seismic coefficient is applied to all masses above the foundation. The freeboard check (soffit vs. DWL) must appear as an explicit computation in the source XLS; it is not implied by the high-level designation alone.

C — Abutment Design (Type 1 — Gravity, IRC:78-1983)
ABUTMENT (IRC:78-1983) — TYPE 1 GRAVITY: The Banas River high-level bridge has a single combined source XLS ("HFL BRIDGE BANAS RIVER WITH OPEN FOUNDATION R-1.xls") covering stability, scour, and abutment. The abutment is a gravity (Type 1) mass concrete structure in PCC grade M20 (IRC:78-1983 Cl. 702.1 specifies minimum M15; M20 adopted for the sandy Banas River environment and durability) — appropriate if abutment height H ≤ 4.5 m. Banas River at this crossing has a wide sandy bed (Lacey waterway governs); the approach embankment height is modest. Active earth pressure per Rankine Ka = (1−sinφ)/(1+sinφ); live-load surcharge hs = 1.2 m per IRC:78-1983 Cl. 706.2. The three stability checks — sliding (FOS ≥ 1.50), overturning (FOS ≥ 1.80), and bearing pressure (FOS ≥ 2.50 on SBC) — must be confirmed in the R-1 XLS. If H exceeds 4.5 m due to the sandy-bed scour depth requiring a deep foundation, upgrade to Type 2 Cantilever RCC abutment.
