A — Project Context: Ayad River Urban Crossings
THE AYAD RIVER IN UDAIPUR — URBAN HYDROLOGY: The Ayad is one of the few perennial urban streams in Rajasthan, fed by the overflow of Fateh Sagar Lake and the outfall of Udaipur's stormwater drainage network. Its flood behaviour at the Maharashtra Bhawan crossing is complex: the design HFL is not simply a function of catchment rainfall, but is also controlled by the lake's Full Supply Level (266.77 m MSL, Fateh Sagar datum), its spillway gate openings, and the backwater effect from downstream constrictions. Urban bridges across the Ayad must therefore be designed to the municipal authority's certified HFL, which typically exceeds the HFL derived by Manning's equation alone. The Maharashtra Bhawan – New Bhupalpura crossing is also subject to the constraints of the urban road geometry (lane widths, carriageway gradient, kerb alignment) which limit the available span arrangement and impose specific abutment height requirements.
URBAN BRIDGE CONTEXT: The Ayad River flows through the urban area of Udaipur city. Multiple crossings exist along its alignment, and the Attached_Assets folder contains design source files for at least three of them: (i) the Maharashtra Bhawan – New Bhupalpura submersible bridge (this report); (ii) the Old Bhupalpura – New Bhupalpura submersible bridge (see separate report); and (iii) the Fatehpura–Bhuwana high-level bridge (see separate report). For urban bridges, the SBC is typically lower due to filled urban ground, and HFL is influenced by upstream impoundments (e.g., Fateh Sagar, Pichola). The checking engineer must confirm the HFL used in the design against the Udaipur municipal authority's certified flood level, not the generic Rajasthan PWD standard.

B — Hydraulic Analysis Summary (from Hydraulic Calculations DOC)
HYDRAULIC ANALYSIS (IRC SP-13): The "Hydraulic Calculations AYAD BRIDGE.doc" (Git LFS) establishes the HFL, design discharge, velocity, afflux, and scour depth for the Ayad River at this crossing. The Ayad is an urban stream subject to both natural floods and urban stormwater contributions. Manning's equation is applied to the channel cross-section as surveyed. The Lacey regime width governs the waterway, and the Molesworth formula gives the afflux. IRC:78-1983 scour depth fixes the foundation level. As a submersible bridge, buoyancy and current forces govern; no freeboard check is required. The general "hydraulics.xls" template may be used as the calculation aid for this hydraulic analysis.
