A — Type 1: Gravity Abutment (Mass Concrete / PCC) — IRC:78-1983
DEFINITION & APPLICABILITY: A gravity abutment relies entirely on its own dead weight to resist the active earth pressure, live-load surcharge, and braking/longitudinal forces transmitted from the superstructure. No tensile stress is permitted in the body; the material is plain cement concrete (PCC) grade M15 to M20, or well-burnt brick masonry in cement mortar. Gravity abutments are economical for abutment heights H ≤ 4–5 m (wall height above foundation). Beyond this height, the base width required for overturning stability becomes excessive and a cantilever type becomes more economical. In the collection, all submersible bridges with dedicated abutment workbooks (Bedach, T01, Jakham, Som-Larathi) use the gravity type; kumbhalgarh bridge uses PCC gravity abutments with and without bearings.
DESIGN BASIS (IRC:78-1983): Active earth pressure is calculated by Rankine's theory: Ka = (1 − sin φ)/(1 + sin φ). For typical cohesionless backfill φ = 30°, Ka = 0.333. The resultant horizontal force per unit length = ½ · Ka · γ · H² (kN/m), acting at H/3 from the base. Live-load surcharge is represented by an equivalent fill height hs = 1.2 m (IRC:78 Cl. 706.2) producing a uniform horizontal pressure Ka · γ · hs over the full height. Three failure modes are checked at the base of the abutment:
FOS(sliding) = ΣV · μ / ΣH ≥ 1.50 (μ = 0.50 for PCC on soil)
FOS(overturning) = ΣM_restoring / ΣM_overturning ≥ 1.80
Bearing pressure = ΣV / B ± 6·ΣM_net / B² ≤ Safe Bearing Capacity (SBC)
PROPORTIONING RULES (IRC:78-1983 Cl. 706.1): Top width ≥ 0.6 m; base width B ≈ 0.5–0.7 × H for gravity; batter on back face ≈ 1 in 4 to 1 in 6. No reinforcement required. Minimum cover: not applicable (unreinforced). Self-weight density: 22 kN/m³ (PCC), 19 kN/m³ (brick masonry). The drawing DXF/DWG file must show: front elevation, side elevation, plan at foundation, material note, founding level, and HFL / road level datum.

B — Type 2: Cantilever Abutment (RCC T-Wall / L-Wall) — IRC:78-1983 + IRC:112-2015
DEFINITION & APPLICABILITY: A cantilever abutment is an RCC wall shaped like an inverted T (or L). The vertical stem cantilevers from the base slab; the heel slab retains the backfill by its own weight and the weight of the fill above it; the toe slab provides additional bearing area and moment arm. This type becomes more economical than gravity when H ≥ 4–5 m because the weight of the retained soil, rather than the abutment concrete, provides the stabilising force. For high-level bridges where approach embankments are tall, and for any bridge where the founding level is deep, the cantilever type is preferred. In this collection, the Sisarama Nalah high-level bridge project explicitly uses the cantilever RCC retaining wall ("DESIGN OF CANTILEVER RCC RETAINING WALL.xls"). All high-level bridges with significant fill height should adopt Type 2 or Type 3.
DESIGN BASIS (IRC:78-1983 + IRC:112-2015): Stability checks (sliding, overturning, bearing) are identical to Type 1 but with the self-weight of the T-section and the weight of the fill on the heel slab contributing to ΣV. Stem design (IRC:112-2015): The stem acts as a vertical cantilever fixed at the base slab. The bending moment at the base of the stem = Ka · γ · H³/6 + Ka · γ · hs · H²/2. The shear at the base = Ka · γ · H²/2 + Ka · γ · hs · H. Toe and heel slab design: The toe slab is a cantilever loaded by net upward soil pressure minus self-weight. The heel slab is a cantilever loaded by the weight of fill above it minus net upward pressure. All sections verified for flexure (M_Ed ≤ M_Rd), shear (V_Ed ≤ V_Rd), and minimum reinforcement per IRC:112-2015.
Stem moment at base: M = Ka·γ·H³/6 + Ka·γ·hs·H²/2 (kN·m/m)
Stem shear at base: V = Ka·γ·H²/2 + Ka·γ·hs·H (kN/m)
Heel net pressure: p_heel = ΣV/B + 6·e·ΣV/B² (upward, −γ·fill above heel)
Toe moment: M_toe = p_toe_net · L_toe²/2 (kN·m/m)

C — Type 3: Counterfort Abutment (RCC with Counterforts) — IRC:78-1983 + IRC:112-2015
DEFINITION & APPLICABILITY: A counterfort abutment is an RCC wall in which vertical fins (counterforts) are cast monolithically with the stem and base slab at regular intervals of 3–4 m (typically 0.3–0.5 × H). The front wall (stem) spans horizontally between counterforts as a two-way or one-way slab, rather than cantilevereing over its full height. This eliminates the large bending moment at the base of the stem that makes a simple cantilever uneconomical for H > 7–8 m. The counterforts act as deep T-beams resisting the total horizontal earth pressure from the tributary width. No project in the current collection explicitly uses counterfort abutments; however, any future bridge with approach embankments producing abutment walls taller than 7 m — such as valley crossings for the Parwan River or kumbhalgarh lake road approaches — should evaluate this type. A counterfort type must be designed and drawn before the project is submitted for regulatory approval if H exceeds 7 m.
DESIGN BASIS (IRC:78-1983 + IRC:112-2015): Counterfort spacing L_c = 0.4H to 0.5H (equal to or less than the wall height × 0.5 to avoid wall spans that negate the advantage). Front wall (stem panel): horizontal span = L_c; vertical span = H; boundary conditions: fixed at base slab, pinned at top (if unrestrained). For L_c / H ≤ 2, two-way slab analysis (IS:456 / IRC:112). Bending moments: M_horiz = w·L_c²/8, M_vert = w·H²/8 (w = triangular earth pressure intensity at the level considered). Counterfort: acts as a T-beam with the front wall as flange and the counterfort rib as web. Tension force in the counterfort at any level = horizontal force from tributary area = K_a·γ·z·L_c (kN/m²·m·m = kN) — resisted by the reinforcement in the counterfort.
Counterfort spacing: L_c ≤ 0.5·H (m)
Front wall moment: M_h = Ka·γ·z·L_c²/8 (horizontal, at mid-span between CFTs)
Counterfort tension: T_cft = Ka·γ·z·L_c (kN/m height) → integrated over tributary height
Stability checks: same as Type 1 & 2 — FOS ≥ 1.50/1.80; p_max ≤ SBC

D — Abutment Type Selection Matrix — Both Bridge Categories
SELECTION RULE (IRC:78-1983 Cl. 706): The choice of abutment type depends primarily on wall height H, soil conditions, and project economics. For submersible bridges the abutment height is generally low (H = 2–4 m) because the deck is near the ground level; gravity (Type 1) almost always suffices. For high-level bridges, approach embankments push the wall height up; cantilever (Type 2) may be required from H = 4.5 m; counterfort (Type 3) from H = 7.5 m.

PARAMETRIC GENERATOR — HOW DESIGN PARAMETERS WIRE TO DXF OUTPUT: The src/generators/dxf_builder.py script in the repository accepts design parameters (span, HFL, pier dimensions, abutment type, SBC) from the stability XLS and produces audit-ready DXF drawings via the ezdxf library. The generators follow this pipeline: (1) Read pier geometry and RL data from the stability XLS → (2) Select template from src/generators/templates.py based on bridge type (slab/Tee-beam/box culvert) → (3) Insert IRC-standard title block from src/generators/blocks.py → (4) Write to COMPONENT_DRAWINGS_SORTED/<category>/. For bridge-specific drawing generation: run python main_app.py and select the bridge from the project list. The Smart Terrain Engine auto-calculates pier/abutment heights from NSL data input.
