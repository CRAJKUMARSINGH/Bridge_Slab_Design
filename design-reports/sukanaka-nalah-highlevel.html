A — Project Scope: High-Level vs. Submersible Alternative
THE ECONOMICS OF THE HIGH-LEVEL / SUBMERSIBLE CHOICE: The Sukanaka Nalah project is one of only two projects in this collection (the other being Sisarama Nalah) where both a high-level and a submersible design have been explicitly developed and documented side-by-side. This is a rare and valuable comparative dataset. The trade-off is well-understood in bridge engineering: a submersible bridge costs approximately 40–60% less per running metre than a high-level bridge of equivalent span on the same site, but incurs an annual traffic closure of 6–12 weeks during the monsoon. The economic choice depends on traffic volume, road classification, and the value of uninterrupted access. For a seasonal nala like the Sukanaka in a tribal belt location (Udaipur–Jhamar Kotada Road), where traffic volumes are moderate and the alignment serves primarily agricultural communities with rural road category loading, the submersible option is often the rational choice. The existence of a high-level design indicates that the project authority evaluated the higher option as well — perhaps in response to a government directive to upgrade cross-drainage on the Udaipur–Jhamar Kotada corridor to all-weather standard. The checking engineer must obtain the formal project brief and design authority direction to confirm which alternative has been sanctioned.
DESIGN ALTERNATIVE CONTEXT: The Sukanaka Nalah project has both a submersible bridge design (Matoon Village crossing, separate report) and this high-level bridge design. The existence of both suggests that the design authority evaluated two alternatives — a lower-cost submersible option and a higher-standard high-level option — before selecting the preferred design. The high-level alternative requires the IRC:5-2015 freeboard clearance, which demands a higher deck elevation and consequently more expensive approach embankments and longer span abutments. The checking engineer should compare both alternatives against the project brief to confirm which has been adopted.

B — BEDACH Stability and Freeboard Verification
HIGH-LEVEL BRIDGE BEDACH SEQUENCE (IRC:6-2016): For the high-level alternative, the dead load is the same as for the submersible (slab, wearing coat, cap, pier, footing), but the buoyancy condition during normal non-flood periods is different — the foundation may not be submerged during the typical (non-flood) service condition. The governing load combination for the high-level bridge is likely Case 3 (Wind + Service) or the seismic case, rather than the flood-buoyancy case that governs submersible bridges. The wind load on the deck soffit and parapet is significant because the deck is elevated above the flood plain and fully exposed to the design wind. The seismic case applies horizontal acceleration to all masses — including the elevated deck mass, which has a larger lever arm above the foundation than in a submersible bridge.
FREEBOARD VERIFICATION (IRC:5-2015): The high-level classification requires: Deck Soffit Level − Design Water Level ≥ IRC:5-2015 minimum clearance. The minimum clearance varies by design discharge tier (from 0.30 m to 1.50 m for large river crossings per IRC:5 Table 1). The checking engineer must confirm that the soffit level declared in the stability XLS matches the design cross-section and that the afflux component of the DWL is included. A high-level bridge that fails the freeboard check is not classified as high-level regardless of deck elevation.
