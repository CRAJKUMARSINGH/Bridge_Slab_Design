A — Project Context: Bridge over a Lake
THE KELWARA LAKE CROSSING — A DIFFERENT KIND OF BRIDGE: Most bridges in this collection are designed to resist river floods: the structural challenge is scour undermining foundations, water current forces destabilising piers, and buoyancy reducing effective weight. The Kumbhalgarh bridge is different in every respect. It crosses a lake — Kelwara Lake — where there is essentially no flow velocity (V ≈ 0), no active channel scour, and no Molesworth afflux. The design water level is simply the Full Supply Level (FSL) of the lake, which is controlled by the dam or bund at its outfall. This shifts the dominant structural concerns entirely: water current forces vanish from the BEDACH calculation, scour depth is zero or nominal, and the governing instability cases become wind (because the bridge crosses open lake water with full wind exposure over a long fetch), seismic (horizontal acceleration of the full dead and live load mass), and buoyancy at pier and footing (which is still present because the lake water surrounds the substructure). The Kumbhalgarh–Kelwara Road provides access to Kumbhalgarh Fort (UNESCO World Heritage Site), so the bridge has a significant tourism and economic role beyond its rural connectivity function.
PROJECT IDENTITY: The Kumbhalgarh bridge crosses Kelwara Lake on the Kelwara–Kumbhalgarh Road. Kumbhalgarh Fort, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is located on this road. The crossing over a lake rather than a river presents different hydraulic challenges: the "flood level" is effectively the full supply level of the lake, the flow velocity is negligible, and the governing structural concern shifts from scour and current force to buoyancy, wind, and seismic stability. The NABARD funding source indicates this is a rural road development project for tribal area connectivity.
PCC ABUTMENT NOTE: Two source workbooks address the abutment: "DESIGN OF PCC ABUTMENTS WITH OPEN FOUNDATION-KUMBHALGARH.xls" and "KUMBHALGARH Abutment.xls" (and a variant "KUMBHALGARH ABUTMENT WITHOUT BEARING.xls"). The PCC abutment is plain cement concrete without reinforcement — this is appropriate for the gravity-mass stabilisation approach at locations with competent founding strata. The "without bearing" variant indicates a comparison study with a direct bearing arrangement versus a elastomeric pad bearing. The Neelkanth Mahadev Final estimate provides the BOQ for NABARD submission.

B — PCC Abutment Design and Stability Narrative
PCC GRAVITY ABUTMENT (IRC:78-1983) — CONCRETE GRADE M20: The plain cement concrete gravity abutment achieves stability through mass — no steel reinforcement is provided in the main body. Concrete grade: PCC M20 (IS:456 / IRC:78-1983 Cl. 702.1; minimum M15 permitted for gravity abutments, M20 adopted here for durability in the lake-side environment with possible water contact at submerged levels). The design checks: (i) Sliding FOS = (μ × V) / H ≥ 1.50, where μ is the base friction coefficient, V the total vertical load, and H the total horizontal destabilising force; (ii) Overturning FOS = M_restoring / M_overturning ≥ 1.80; (iii) Bearing pressure p_max ≤ SBC, p_min ≥ 0 (tensile contact not permitted in PCC). The "without bearing" variant examines the direct slab-on-abutment cap arrangement without elastomeric pads, which changes the distribution of superstructure load reaction and the effective eccentricity at the base.
