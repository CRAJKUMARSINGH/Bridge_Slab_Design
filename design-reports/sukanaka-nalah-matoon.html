DESIGN ITERATION NARRATIVE: The existence of two stability XLS variants and two dirt wall XLS variants for the Sukanaka Nalah submersible bridge at Matoon Village is itself a telling piece of engineering history. Iterative design in bridge engineering typically occurs when the first-pass geometry fails one or more stability checks — most commonly, when the computed foundation bearing pressure p_max exceeds the adopted SBC under the governing BEDACH load case. The engineer must then revise pier dimensions, footing size, or foundation depth, and re-run all nine load cases. The two variants here — one labelled "NEAR M" (likely a preliminary truncated title) and one labelled "MATOON VILLAGE" (the definitive final name) — almost certainly represent this sequence: an initial design followed by a revision. The checking engineer must establish which variant is the accepted final design before issuing drawings.
PROJECT IDENTITY: This submersible bridge carries the Udaipur–Jhamar Kotada road across the Sukanaka Nalah near Matoon Village. The Sukanaka is a seasonal nala in the tribal belt south of Udaipur. The project has an unusually rich source-file set: two variants of the stability analysis (indicating an iterative design process), two variants of the dirt wall design, one abutment design workbook, and a design notes DOC. Both stability XLS variants are stored in Git LFS and should be compared by the checking engineer to identify which represents the accepted final design.
