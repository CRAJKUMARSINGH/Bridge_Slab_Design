A — Design Basis and Hydraulic Summary
RIVER AND ROAD CONTEXT: The Bedach River rises in the Aravalli hills north of Udaipur and flows eastward through the Banas basin. At the Syphon Tiraha – Bedla crossing, the Bedach occupies a moderate-width alluvial channel with a sandy bed and a bed slope that places it in the Lacey sub-critical regime. The Syphon Tiraha – Bedla Road is a district-level connector that links the urban outskirts of Udaipur to the Bedla agricultural hinterland; this bridge is the only all-weather crossing of the Bedach on this road, making its year-round trafficability strategically significant for agricultural transport and emergency access. The submersible design accepts seasonal overtopping during the July–September monsoon; this is economically rational because the frequency of traffic closure is offset by the substantially lower construction cost versus a high-level bridge.
PROJECT IDENTITY: This bridge carries traffic across the Bedach River on the Syphon Tiraha – Bedla Road. Four source workbooks cover the complete design: (i) Stability Analysis XLS — pier stability across all load combinations; (ii) LLCALpier1 XLS — live load computation for pier line reactions; (iii) Abutment Design XLS — gravity abutment check for sliding, overturning, and bearing; (iv) Dirt Wall Design XLS — dirt wall section adequacy. The DRAWING NOTES BEDACH DOCX provides sketching notes for the accompanying drawings. All binary source files are tracked in Git LFS.
HYDRAULIC ANALYSIS (IRC SP-13): The Bedach River at this location has a recorded HFL from site surveys. Manning's equation Q = A·(R^2/3·S^1/2)/n is applied to the adopted cross-section to confirm the design discharge. The Lacey regime width, afflux (Molesworth), and IRC:78-1983 scour depth are computed in sequence. Since this is a submersible bridge, the deck is designed to overtop; buoyancy forces and scour resistance govern rather than freeboard. The design velocity and current forces at 20° skew per IRC:6 Cl. 213.5 are tabulated in the stability workbook.

B — BEDACH Load Sequence: Pier Stability (All 9 Cases)
STEP A — DEAD LOAD (IRC:6-2016 Cl. 203): Dead load includes superstructure slab, wearing course, pier cap, pier shaft (rectangular and curved portions), and footing. With 100% buoyancy at design flood, effective pier weight uses γ_submerged = γ_conc − γ_water = 24 − 10 = 14 kN/m³ for the footing. The pier shaft applies the 15% effective buoyancy factor per IRC:78-1983.
STEPS B–E — LIVE LOAD, WATER CURRENT, SEISMIC, WIND: Live load reactions are extracted from the LLCALpier1 XLS. Water current forces at 20° skew per IRC:6 Cl. 213.5 are computed using the 52·k·v²·A/100 formula for each elevation zone (deck, pier cap, pier shaft). Seismic: Zone II — not governing (IRC:6 Cl. 222.1). Wind per IRC:6 Cl. 212.3 applied to all exposed surfaces.

C — Abutment Design and Dirt Wall Check
ABUTMENT (IRC:6-2016, IRC:78-1983): The gravity abutment is checked for sliding (FOS ≥ 1.50), overturning (FOS ≥ 1.80), and bearing (FOS ≥ 2.50) per the "Design of ABUTMENT SUBMERSIBLE BRIDGE ACROSS BEDACH RIVER" XLS. Active earth pressure per Rankine theory with the adopted φ and γ. Surcharge from live load over the fill. Earth pressure lever arm at H/3 above base. All results in source XLS (LFS).
DIRT WALL (IRC:6-2016): The dirt wall between the approach slab and abutment cap is checked for bending, shear, and bearing on the cap. Source: "Design of Dirt Wall SUBMERSIBLE BRIDGE ACROSS BEDACH RIVER" XLS (LFS). Reinforcement provided must satisfy IRC:112-2015 limits for minimum steel and crack width.
