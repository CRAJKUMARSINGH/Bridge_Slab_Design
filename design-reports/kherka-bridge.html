A — Project Identity: Tee-Beam Bridge Analysis
WHY TEE-BEAMS FOR 19 M? For spans below 8–10 m, a solid RCC slab bridge is structurally efficient and economical — the slab acts monolithically in two directions. Beyond approximately 12 m, the self-weight of a solid slab becomes disproportionately large relative to the live load it carries, and the flexural depth needed to control deflection makes solid slab construction wasteful. The tee-beam (T-beam) bridge solves this by concentrating longitudinal bending capacity in the stems of the T-beams, which are deep and stiff but narrow, while the deck slab spanning between them transversely remains thin. At 19 m — well above the solid-slab economic limit — the Kherka Bridge correctly adopts the tee-beam arrangement. The Courbon method, used here for lateral live-load distribution, was the standard pre-computer method for bridges with up to about 5 longitudinal beams and spans up to approximately 24 m. It assumes a rigid bridge deck (infinite transverse stiffness) and applies a linear distribution formula — valid for the Kherka geometry but progressively unconservative for wider bridges or bridges with flexible decks, where grillage or finite-element analysis would be needed. The checking engineer should confirm that the rigid-deck assumption is satisfied by the deck slab stiffness relative to the beam stiffness.
PROJECT SCOPE: The Kherka (also spelled Kharka) Bridge is a 19 m span tee-beam bridge. Unlike the slab bridges that form the majority of the collection, a tee-beam bridge uses longitudinal T-beams as the primary structural members, with a deck slab spanning between them transversely. This requires a two-stage analysis: (i) transverse analysis of the deck slab as a continuous slab over the beam stems; (ii) longitudinal analysis of each tee-beam for bending and shear. The Courbon method is used for lateral distribution of vehicle loads to individual beams.

B — Courbon Method Analysis and Limit State Design Narrative
COURBON'S METHOD — LATERAL LOAD DISTRIBUTION: Courbon's method distributes the vehicle transverse position to individual beams using a linear variation formula: R_i = P × [1/n ± (e × x_i / Σx²)], where P is the total load, n is the number of beams, e is the eccentricity of the vehicle from the bridge centreline, and x_i is the distance of beam i from the centroid of the beam system. The maximum bending moment per beam = R_i × mid-span moment due to IRC loading on the governing span arrangement. This method is applicable for bridges up to approximately 24 m span with reasonable torsional stiffness.
DECK SLAB DESIGN (IRC:112-2015): The deck slab spanning between the tee-beam webs is designed as a continuous slab for the negative (hogging) moment over the beams and positive (sagging) moment at mid-panel. The "DETAILED SLAB DESIGN.htm" stored in Attached_Assets (as a STRUDS-style reference output, tracked in Git LFS) provides an example of the IRC:21 / IRC:112 slab design output format. Minimum slab thickness, clear cover, bar spacing, and crack-width requirements per IRC:112-2015 Annexure A apply.
TEE-BEAM DESIGN (IRC:112-2015): Each tee-beam is designed for the governing Courbon distribution factor, giving the design bending moment M_Ed at mid-span and the design shear force V_Ed at the support. Flexural design: M_Ed ≤ M_Rd = 0.87 f_y A_st (d − 0.42 x_u) in the simplified approach. Shear design: V_Ed ≤ V_Rd per IRC:112-2015 Cl. 10.3. Flange width of T-beam per IRC:21 / IRC:112 effective-width rules. Longitudinal shear at the slab-beam interface must also be checked.
FREEBOARD CHECK (IRC:5-2015) — MANDATORY FOR HIGH-LEVEL CLASSIFICATION: Although the Kherka Bridge is primarily documented as a structural analysis (Courbon method + IRC:112-2015 limit state), it is classified as a High-Level Bridge. This means the deck soffit must satisfy the mandatory IRC:5-2015 Table 1 minimum clearance above the Design Water Level (HFL + Molesworth afflux). The minimum freeboard ranges from 0.30 m (small nala, Q < 0.3 m³/s) up to 1.50 m (large river, Q > 3000 m³/s). The HFL, design discharge, and deck soffit level must be documented in the source design report DOCX ("DESIGN REPORT KHARKA BRIDGE.docx") with an explicit freeboard computation. A high-level bridge whose soffit does not satisfy IRC:5-2015 Table 1 clearance cannot be submitted as a high-level structure, regardless of deck elevation. The checking engineer must confirm this calculation exists in the source DOCX before approving the drawings.

C — Abutment Design (Type 2 — Cantilever RCC, IRC:78-1983 + IRC:112-2015)
ABUTMENT (IRC:78-1983 + IRC:112-2015) — TYPE 2 CANTILEVER RCC: The Kherka Bridge is a 19 m span tee-beam bridge on a high-level alignment. Tee-beam bridges of this span require a significant deck height above the HFL; the resulting approach embankment height typically exceeds 4.5 m, making a Type 2 cantilever RCC abutment the preferred IRC:78-1983 type. The FINAL BRIDGE KHERKA report DOCX files (Git LFS) contain the structural analysis; the abutment design should be extracted from the Design Report DOCX and cross-referenced to the 19 m span Courbon-distribution pier reactions. The stem is designed per IRC:112-2015 for the active earth pressure moment at the base; the toe and heel slabs are checked for net bearing pressure. Active earth pressure: Rankine Ka. LL surcharge hs = 1.2 m per IRC:78 Cl. 706.2. Stability: FOS ≥ 1.50 (sliding), ≥ 1.80 (OT), bearing ≤ SBC (FOS ≥ 2.50).
